# Nashville property standards: 73.8 percent of unpermitted-STR complaints sit on parcels with no STR permit record at all

**One-line finding:** Of the 568 property standards cases in Nashville's layer whose reported problem is recorded with the exact string "host - no strp", 419 (73.8 percent) sit on parcels that have no short-term rental permit record of any status in Metro's STR permits file, and 38 (6.7 percent) sit on parcels with a currently issued STR permit.

Source: Metro Nashville open data, Property Standards Violations layer (Property_Standards_Violations_2, 43,283 records pulled November 1, 2026), maintained by the Metro Codes Department, fused on parcel (APN) to the Metro STR permits file (17,634 records pulled the same day, the same ArcGIS view used for the October 14, 2026 Machine Readable STR permits brief). Cases were received from October 26, 2023 to October 29, 2026.

Three definitions govern everything below. First, the unit of observation is the case: one row is one property standards case as recorded in the layer, and a parcel or address can carry many cases. Second, the STR permit classes describe the permit file as pulled on November 1, 2026, not the permit status on the day a case was received: a parcel classed "expired only" may have held a live permit when an earlier case was filed, and a parcel classed "currently issued" may have been permitted after the case. The classes are printed with every fusion figure so that limit travels with the numbers. Third, "host - no strp" is one exact string in a free-text field; near-miss spellings are counted separately in Finding 7 and are not folded into the 568.

## Finding 1: The layer holds 43,283 cases over a three-year window, 93.0 percent of them recorded DONE

The layer holds 43,283 cases, pulled in full on November 1, 2026 (the server count and the local count are equal; the count was also 43,283 when the service was sampled on October 29, 2026, and the service reports its data last edited November 1, 2026). Date_Received is a date-only field (every value is midnight America/Chicago) and runs from October 26, 2023 to October 29, 2026, so 2023 is a partial year beginning October 26 and 2026 runs through October 29. Cases by received year: 1,936 in partial 2023, 13,807 in 2024, 13,936 in 2025, and 13,604 in 2026 through October 29.

The cases sit on 28,863 distinct parcels (APN present on all 43,283 rows) and 29,031 distinct normalized addresses, and carry 43,281 distinct request numbers (2 rows share a request number with another row). Status as recorded at pull time: DONE 40,245 cases (93.0 percent), OPEN 2,988 (6.9 percent), CLOSED 50 (0.1 percent). The layer does not publish a definition distinguishing DONE from CLOSED, and this brief reports the strings as recorded without merging them.

The earliest received date is three years before the latest, to the week. The layer's published description does not state a retention rule, so this brief describes the window as pulled (October 26, 2023 to October 29, 2026) and makes no claim about cases received before October 26, 2023, which do not appear in the layer.

## Finding 2: Two case types carry 82.2 percent of the caseload; the recorded violations are led by high weeds, open storage, and parking on grass

By the layer's case-type field (Subtype_Description, 18 recorded values), the caseload is: Codes Housing Request 27,495 cases (63.5 percent), Codes High Grass and Weeds 8,075 (18.7 percent), PS Audit 3,824 (8.8 percent), Short Term Rental Property 1,491 (3.4 percent), CA Codes Case 999 (2.3 percent), and Codes Sign Request 692 (1.6 percent), with the remaining 12 types at 307 cases or fewer each. The full counter is in the package's audit output.

By complaint source, cases arrive through HUB (hubNashville) 16,462 (38.0 percent), INSPECTOR REPORTED 16,100 (37.2 percent), PHONE 4,217 (9.7 percent), Audit 3,800 (8.8 percent), and EMAIL 1,872 (4.3 percent), with 13 further sources at 462 or fewer.

By the violations an inspector recorded (Violations_Noted, exact strings; a case can record several violations in one combined string, so these are counts of cases carrying each exact recorded string, not shares of a whole), the largest are HIGH WEEDS (8,564 cases), OPEN STORAGE, JUNK, TRASH, DEBRIS (6,154), PARKING ON GRASS (5,639), and STRP PERMIT REQUIRED (1,146 cases with that exact string; 1,157 cases carry it alone or in a combined string).

One field cannot support problem-type rankings: Reported_Problem is free text holding 21,721 distinct values across 43,283 cases (86 cases carry no value), mixing intake shorthand codes, variant spellings of the same problem, and pasted hubNashville report text. Every Reported_Problem figure in this brief is therefore an exact-string count, printed as such; the case-type field above is the layer's structured alternative.

## Finding 3: Case counts concentrate in two southern ZIP codes and spread across council districts; the sources carry no housing denominator, so no district or ZIP rate is computed

By ZIP code (determined on 43,276 cases; ZIP is null on 2 cases and carries a sentinel 0 on 5, and those 7 stay in every denominator in this brief), two ZIP codes hold 31.2 percent of all cases: 37013 with 7,226 cases (16.7 percent) and 37211 with 6,267 (14.5 percent), followed by 37115 with 3,260 (7.5 percent) and 37207 with 3,091 (7.1 percent). By council district (determined on 43,281 cases; district is null on 2), the largest counts are District 29 with 2,571 cases (5.9 percent), District 11 with 2,427 (5.6 percent), and District 16 with 2,382 (5.5 percent); no district exceeds 5.9 percent.

Exposure caveat, stated prominently because it governs what this finding can mean: these are counts and shares of cases, not rates. Neither source carries a housing-unit, parcel, or population denominator by ZIP code or council district, so this brief does not compute cases per housing unit and does not rank districts or ZIP codes by enforcement intensity. A district with more cases may have more housing, more reporting, more inspector-initiated audits, or more violations; the layer cannot separate those, and no causal pattern is implied.

## Finding 4: Half of all cases sit at addresses that appear more than once

Grouping cases by normalized property address, 20,830 addresses carry exactly 1 case and 8,201 addresses carry 2 or more. The 22,453 cases at repeat addresses are 51.9 percent of all cases. At the top of the distribution, 332 addresses carry 6 or more cases, holding 2,541 cases between them, and the largest single address count is 39 cases. On the parcel key the pattern is the same: 22,673 cases sit on the 8,253 parcels (APN) that carry 2 or more cases. This brief does not name individual addresses or parcels: repeat counts mix repeat complaints about one condition, separate conditions at one property over three years, and multi-unit properties whose units share one recorded address, and the layer cannot separate those.

## Finding 5: The open share is 17.5 percent for cases received in 2026 and 1.5 to 2.6 percent for earlier years; that is a snapshot pattern, not a resolution rate

Status is a snapshot at the November 1, 2026 pull. The share of cases still OPEN by received year is 1.7 percent for partial 2023 (33 of 1,936), 1.5 percent for 2024 (209 of 13,807), 2.6 percent for 2025 (366 of 13,936), and 17.5 percent for 2026 (2,380 of 13,604). Cases received in 2026 had between 3 days and 10 months in the system at pull time; earlier years' cases had one to three years. The layer carries a last-activity date but no received-to-closed duration field is published in a form this brief could verify, so no resolution-time figure is computed and the open-share gradient is reported as the snapshot pattern it is.

## Finding 6: STR cases are 3.4 percent of the caseload, arrive almost entirely by EMAIL, and concentrate in different districts than the caseload as a whole

The Short Term Rental Property case type holds 1,491 cases (3.4 percent of all cases), received as 77 in partial 2023, 551 in 2024, 519 in 2025, and 344 in 2026 through October 29. Monthly counts inside the window swing from 3 cases (May 2026) to 102 (July 2026), a pattern consistent with batch intake; no month-to-month trend is claimed.

The intake channel differs sharply from the caseload as a whole: 1,463 of the 1,491 STR cases (98.1 percent) carry complaint source EMAIL, against 4.3 percent of all cases. Status: DONE 1,382 (92.7 percent), OPEN 109 (7.3 percent). An STRP PERMIT REQUIRED violation is recorded on 1,156 of the 1,491 cases (77.5 percent), alone or in a combined string.

The geography also differs. The largest STR-case districts are District 21 (191 cases), District 17 (184), and District 5 (132), none of which is among the three largest districts overall (Finding 3). The largest STR-case ZIP codes are 37207 (179 cases), 37208 (173), 37209 (143), and 37203 (128), while the caseload overall is led by 37013 and 37211 (Finding 3). These are case counts on two different populations, printed side by side as counts only; per Finding 3, no rate comparison is made.

## Finding 7: "host - no strp" is an exact-string count of 568 cases, and every one of them records an STRP PERMIT REQUIRED violation

Within the STR case type, the largest single exact Reported_Problem string is "host - no strp" with 568 cases, followed by "host - expired strp" with 108. The 568 was verified against the full November 1 pull for this brief; a count of 572 recorded from a live sample on October 29, 2026 does not reproduce in the full pull and is not used. Spelling and spacing variants of the same shorthand exist ("host- no strp" 7 cases, "Host - no strp" 3, and 6 further variant strings with 8 cases between them): counting every case-and-spacing-normalized variant gives 586, of which the exact string is 568. This brief uses the exact string throughout and prints the variant total here so the boundary is visible.

The 568 cases have a uniform recorded shape: all 568 arrived by EMAIL, all 568 carry the case type Short Term Rental Property, and all 568 record Violations_Noted as exactly STRP PERMIT REQUIRED. Their status is DONE 522 (91.9 percent) and OPEN 46 (8.1 percent), and the largest last-activity result is Violation Resolved (518 cases). They sit on 540 distinct parcels; 25 parcels carry 2 or more of these cases, holding 53 of the 568.

## Finding 8: Fused to the STR permits file, most unpermitted-STR complaints sit on parcels with no permit record at all, and STR cases sit on currently permitted parcels at about 50 times the control rate

The fusion layer is Metro's STR permits file as published for the October 14, 2026 STR permits brief and pulled fresh for this brief: 17,634 records, of which 7,010 carry status ISSUED, sitting on 5,466 distinct parcels; 10,742 distinct parcels appear in the file at any status. The join is Property_APN to Parcel, alphanumeric-normalized. APN is present on 100 percent of rows in both files, and both files use the same two parcel formats (11-character standard and 14-character condo). Of the 28,863 distinct case parcels, 1,378 (4.8 percent) match a parcel in the permits file at any status.

Case-level results, with permit status as recorded at the November 1, 2026 pull (the snapshot limit in the definitions above applies to every figure in this finding):

- All 43,283 cases: 714 (1.6 percent) sit on a parcel with a currently issued STR permit; 868 (2.0 percent) on a parcel whose permit records are expired only; 199 (0.5 percent) on a parcel with permit records of other statuses only; and 41,502 (95.9 percent) on a parcel with no STR permit record at all.
- The 1,491 STR cases: 466 (31.3 percent) sit on a currently issued parcel; 288 (19.3 percent) on an expired-only parcel; 104 (7.0 percent) on an other-status-only parcel; and 633 (42.5 percent) on a parcel with no STR permit record at all.
- The 568 "host - no strp" cases: 419 (73.8 percent) sit on a parcel with no STR permit record at all; 79 (13.9 percent) on an expired-only parcel; 32 (5.6 percent) on an other-status-only parcel; and 38 (6.7 percent) on a currently issued parcel.

Control groups, run to test whether the APN join produces artifacts: cases of the Codes High Grass and Weeds type sit on currently issued STR parcels at 0.51 percent (41 of 8,075 cases), Codes Housing Request cases at 0.66 percent (182 of 27,495), and Codes Sign Request cases at 0.29 percent (2 of 692, a group under the size this brief would normally compare, printed because it is a control). The STR case type's 31.3 percent is about 50 times the housing-request control rate on the same join. An address-based control agrees with the parcel join: 1,637 cases match an STR permit address on normalized address alone, 1,802 match on parcel or address, and 1,616 of those 1,802 (89.7 percent) match on both keys. Address matching understates matches where a permit record names a unit the case record does not, which is why the parcel key is primary.

Layering admission: without the STR permits layer, this brief could not state what share of unpermitted-STR complaints sit on parcels with no permit record at all (73.8 percent), nor that STR enforcement geography differs from the permitted footprint's case pattern; the layer is retained for those claims and for no other.

## What this might mean

One reading of Finding 8 is that the "host - no strp" category is measuring what its name says: nearly three quarters of those complaints (419 of 568) concern parcels that appear nowhere in the permits file at any status, the recorded violation on every one of the 568 is STRP PERMIT REQUIRED, and the residual classes are the ones a permit-history check would predict (expired-only parcels at 13.9 percent, and 38 cases, 6.7 percent, on currently issued parcels, a group small enough to review case by case). On that reading, the fusion also gives the STR program a denominator it did not have in the case layer alone: 1,491 STR cases over three years against a currently issued stock of 7,010 permits on 5,466 parcels.

The strongest alternative explanation is timing. Permit status in this fusion is the status at the November 1, 2026 pull, while cases were received across three years: a parcel classed "currently issued" may have been unpermitted on the day its case was received and permitted afterward, which would move some of the 38 issued-parcel cases into the unpermitted-at-receipt group, and a parcel classed "expired only" may have held a live permit at receipt. The permits file carries issue and expiration dates, so an as-of-receipt reconstruction is possible in principle; this brief did not perform it, because permit records in the file include renewals and duplicate permit numbers whose sequencing the published file does not fully document (the October 14 brief recorded 30 duplicate permit numbers in the file). Until that reconstruction is done, the expired-only and issued shares in Finding 8 are bounds with the direction of the timing effect stated here, not as-of-receipt measurements. A second alternative runs alongside it: a complaint records a reported problem, and while every "host - no strp" case in the layer records an inspector-noted STRP PERMIT REQUIRED violation, the layer does not record whether the property was in fact operated as a short-term rental.

On Finding 6, the alternative explanation for the EMAIL concentration is a single reporting channel rather than a property pattern: if one monitoring service or one intake address files most STR reports, the 98.1 percent EMAIL share describes the reporting pipeline, and the batch-shaped monthly counts (3 to 102) point the same way. The layer does not name the sender, so this brief reports the channel pattern without attributing it.

## Who can act

- **Metro Codes Department, Property Standards Division**, which investigates these cases and owns the case layer, controls the follow-up on the small residual classes. Concrete action: review the 38 "host - no strp" cases that sit on parcels with a currently issued permit and the 79 on expired-only parcels against permit issue and expiration dates, and record for each whether a permit was live on the case's received date. Cost class: low-cost operational change (a records review of 117 cases). Outcome measure: an as-of-receipt permit class for those 117 cases, checkable against this package's data/str_fusion_panel.csv on the next pull.
- **Metro Codes Department with Metro's open data program**, publisher of the layer, controls what the published record can support. Concrete actions: add a structured problem-category field alongside the free-text Reported_Problem (21,721 distinct values in this pull), and publish the layer's retention rule for received dates (the window as pulled begins October 26, 2023). Cost class: low-cost operational change (a derived field and documentation). Outcome measure: the distinct-value count of the structured field on the next pull, and a stated coverage start date in the service description.
- **Metro Codes Department, STR permitting side**, controls the permit file's history. Concrete action: publish a permit status history (or an as-of date field set) that lets a case be fused to the permit status on its received date, resolving the timing limit printed in Finding 8. Cost class: budget decision (a change to the permitting system's published extract). Outcome measure: a repull in which the 568 "host - no strp" cases can be classed as of their received dates, with the 73.8 percent no-record figure recomputed on that basis.
- **Board of Property Standards and Appeals**, which hears and acts on appeals of orders issued under the Property Standards Code (the board's stated function on its Nashville.gov board page), and which is listed on the Nashville.gov event list as meeting on Wednesday, November 4, 2026 at 3:00 PM at the Metro Office Building, controls the appeal outcomes for individual property standards cases. Concrete action: when the as-of-receipt review of the 117 residual cases above is complete, that review is the record against which appealed "host - no strp" cases can be checked. Cost class: low-cost operational change (an existing meeting and an existing records review). Outcome measure: the review covers the 117 cases classed currently issued (38) or expired only (79) in this package's data/str_fusion_panel.csv, and each case's as-of-receipt class is recorded against that panel.
- **Metropolitan Council**, which holds a special meeting on Thursday, November 5, 2026 at 6:30 PM (Nashville.gov news release, October 2, 2026: Vice Mayor Angie E. Henderson called the special meeting because the November 3, 2026 general election falls on the regular meeting date, with associated committee meetings on November 4-5; this brief asserts no STR or property-standards item on that meeting's agenda), controls Codes Department funding and the STR ordinance. Concrete action: where STR enforcement is weighed, weigh it against the fused baseline in Finding 8 (1,491 STR cases in three years; 568 recorded as "host - no strp"; 419 of those on parcels with no permit record) rather than complaint counts alone. ## Data notes

- Snapshot and vintage: all figures are from the pulls of November 1, 2026 (property standards 43,283 records, server count equal to local count, service data last edited November 1, 2026; STR permits 17,634 records, against 17,612 in the October 13, 2026 pull for the October 14 brief). Both layers are updated on a continuing basis, so figures describe the layers as pulled, not any earlier or later state.
- Dates: Date_Received is date-only (midnight America/Chicago on every record); no sentinel years or null received dates appear (audit output). Last_Activity_Date is present on all but 4 records and is used by no figure except the last-activity result counts in Finding 7.
- Category stability: the case-type mix is not stable across the window. CA Codes Case grows from 1 case in partial 2023 to 106 in 2024, 369 in 2025, and 523 in 2026, and PS Audit swings from 289 to 1,732 to 477 to 1,326 across the same years, so year-to-year movement in any single type reflects intake and audit scheduling as well as reported conditions; this brief makes no trend claim for any type. The STR case type is present in every year of the window (77, 551, 519, 344).
- Unit discipline: rows are cases throughout. Parcel figures use distinct APN values; permit figures use permit records or distinct Parcel values, labeled each time. No figure divides cases by parcels or permits by cases.
- Joins: one join only, APN to Parcel (Finding 8), with the address control and the two non-STR control case types recorded there and in the audit. No point-proximity, neighborhood, or street-network join is used. ZIP and district figures are the layer's own fields; ZIP carries a 0 sentinel on 5 cases and is null on 2, district is null on 2, and those not-determined cases stay in every denominator.
- Free-text discipline: Reported_Problem and Violations_Noted are free or combined strings. Counters for both were recorded in full before any figure was computed (audit output and data/reported_problem_counter.csv.gz, data/violations_noted_counter.csv.gz); every figure from them is an exact-string or explicitly labeled contains count.
- Named records: this brief names no individual address, parcel, owner, or operator. Repeat-address figures (Finding 4) are aggregates only.
- Reproduction: code/pull_data.py records both pulls and the server counters; code/audit_section10.py records the pre-flight audit (output in data/audit_output.txt); code/build_stats.py builds stats.json and the derived panels; code/verify_independent.py is the independent recomputation behind verification-ledger-independent.md; code/charts.py draws the charts; code/hero_composite.py composites the hero.
